## Group A — Brief Answer Questions [10 × 2 = 20]

### A1. Define any two types of entrepreneurship

**Innovative entrepreneurship** introduces a new product, service, process, or model and grows by solving a problem differently. It accepts higher uncertainty because the solution is untested. **Social entrepreneurship** uses a sustainable business model to solve an environmental or social problem while earning enough revenue to continue. Its main test is measurable social impact alongside financial sustainability.

*(56 words)*

### A2. What is the concept of creative thinking?

Creative thinking is the deliberate use of original, varied, and useful ideas to solve a problem or create value. It changes how a founder frames the problem, generates options, and tests possible solutions. Techniques include brainstorming, mind mapping, SCAMPER, and design thinking. In entrepreneurship, it prevents founders from accepting the first obvious solution.

*(53 words)*

### A3. Define business model

A business model explains how a venture creates, delivers, and captures value. It connects the target customer and its problem to the product, channels, relationships, activities, resources, partners, revenue, and costs. A strong model earns money because customer value exceeds delivery cost. For an IT startup, it also shows how the product scales and covers technology, people, marketing, and compliance.

*(60 words)*

### A4. What is intellectual property right?

Intellectual property rights are legal rights protecting creations of the mind. They give owners control over commercial use and protection from unauthorised copying. Nepal's main categories are patents for inventions, trademarks for brand names and logos, copyright for original works and software code, and industrial designs for product appearance. Startups should register important assets and protect confidential know-how through contracts.

*(60 words)*

### A5. Define business plan

A business plan is a roadmap explaining a venture's problem, solution, market, model, operations, marketing, financial needs, risks, and funding request. It helps founders test assumptions and align the team. Investors use it to judge whether the idea has a credible market, executable plan, goals, and clear use of funds. A plan states assumptions and updates them as evidence changes.

*(60 words)*

### A6. What is a business ecosystem?

A business ecosystem is the connected network of companies, customers, investors, institutions, infrastructure, and rules supporting activity in a market. Each participant depends on the others, so one weak link can limit the group. A digital-payment ecosystem needs users, merchants, banks, operators, regulators, internet access, and dispute handling. Nepal needs stronger finance and predictable rules for smaller businesses.

*(58 words)*

### A7. What is risk management?

Risk management identifies possible threats, judges their likelihood and impact, and takes proportionate action before losses occur. Startups face market, technical, financial, legal, operational, and team risks. The process uses prevention, reduction, transfer, or acceptance, supported by owners, deadlines, budgets, and contingency plans. It also monitors warning signs and learns after incidents. The aim is to prevent avoidable surprises.

*(59 words)*

### A8. Define labour law

Labour law governs the relationship between employers and workers. It covers recruitment, contracts, wages, working hours, leave, workplace safety, social security, termination, and dispute resolution. In Nepal, the Labour Act 2074 is the main statute, supported by social-security rules. A startup must classify platform workers correctly and document terms fairly, because app-based work can create legal uncertainty.

*(57 words)*

### A9. What is brand building?

Brand building is the process of creating a clear identity, promise, and reputation that customers recognise and trust. It combines a distinctive name and visual design with consistent communication, service quality, and customer experience. A startup defines its audience and positioning, delivers a reliable promise, listens to feedback, and uses suitable channels. Strong brands lower acquisition cost and improve retention.

*(60 words)*

### A10. Mention any two importance of CRM

First, CRM helps a startup understand prospects. It records leads, activities, and conversion data, so founders can stop spending on weak channels. Second, it supports retention by tracking usage, complaints, satisfaction, and renewal signals. The team can contact inactive customers before they leave, improving lifetime value and revenue predictability.

*(60 words)*

## Group B — Short Answer Questions [attempt any SIX] [6 × 5 = 30]

### B1. Discuss the role of ICT for effective entrepreneurship

ICT supports entrepreneurship in five linked ways. **First, it lowers entry cost.** Cloud hosting, open-source software, shared workspaces, and digital payment tools replace large upfront investments in servers, offices, and equipment. **Second, it expands reach.** A small team can sell software or services to customers across Nepal and overseas. **Third, it enables new business models.** Platforms, marketplaces, fintech, SaaS, and digital delivery depend on data networks and software. **Fourth, it speeds learning.** Analytics, online feedback, A/B tests, and rapid prototypes reveal customer preferences before heavy spending. **Fifth, it improves coordination and finance.** Digital accounting, CRM, remote collaboration, and crowdfunding connect founders with staff, customers, and capital. ICT cannot replace product-market fit, sound regulation, or capable people, but it lets a lean startup learn and scale much faster.

ICT also changes the founder's role. Instead of supervising every task, the leader can manage data, experiment, and relationships. A small team can test several ideas at once and keep the best. The limitation is important: digital access is uneven, cyber risk rises, and platforms can depend on a few large suppliers. ICT helps most when the venture pairs it with reliable infrastructure, security, digital literacy, and clear rules for handling customer data.

*(200 words)*

### B2. Explain the steps of prototyping development

Prototyping turns an idea into something users can react to. **1. Identify the riskiest assumption.** Decide whether the main uncertainty is demand, usability, price, or technical feasibility. **2. Define the core experience.** State the user's job and the minimum flow needed to complete it. **3. Choose the lowest-fidelity prototype.** Use paper sketches or a clickable wireframe for flows; use a concierge service or wizard-of-oz delivery when behaviour matters more than technology; build a functional MVP when integration must be tested. **4. Build quickly.** Include only the risky core and record assumptions behind each choice. **5. Test with real users.** Observe users completing the task, collect confusion and failure points, and avoid explaining the prototype. **6. Measure and revise.** Track completion, errors, time, repeat use, and willingness to pay. **7. Decide.** Continue, change, or abandon the assumption before scaling. The prototype is an experiment, not a miniature finished product.

Keep the prototype separate from the final product. Document what was learned, not just what was built. A useful test has a hypothesis, a task, and a success measure. For example, if trust is uncertain, test a concierge booking before building driver matching. If navigation is uncertain, test the route map before optimising payment. This prevents a polished prototype from hiding a weak business idea.

*(213 words)*

### B3. Explain any five building blocks of the Business Model Canvas framework

The Business Model Canvas describes a venture through nine connected blocks. **1. Customer Segments** identifies who receives value, such as commuters, tourists, and drivers. **2. Value Propositions** states the problem solved and why the offer is better: safer, faster, or cheaper. **3. Channels** explains how customers are reached and served through an app, website, referral, or partnership. **4. Customer Relationships** covers acquisition, support, trust, loyalty, and feedback. **5. Revenue Streams** shows how money arrives through commission, subscription, advertising, or transaction fees. Other blocks cover key resources, activities, partnerships, and costs. The canvas is useful because it exposes weak assumptions. If customers are attractive but the revenue stream is unclear, the model is not ready for investment.

The blocks should be drawn in the standard order and tested against evidence rather than treated as nine independent lists. For example, a strong value proposition is weak if the chosen channel cannot reach the segment affordably. Key resources and partnerships expose dependencies, while cost structure tests whether the revenue stream can support the promise. A canvas is finished only when each block connects to the others and the team can state the assumption that needs testing next.

*(194 words)*

### B4. Describe basic legal requirements for startups in Nepal

A Nepali startup should complete a basic legal and compliance setup. **1. Register the business** with the Office of Company Registrar or use the legally appropriate registration route. **2. Obtain tax registration** with the Inland Revenue Department, including PAN and VAT registration when turnover rules require it. **3. Protect intellectual property** through patent, trademark, copyright, or design registration and written employee and contractor agreements. **4. Check sector permission.** A fintech, payment, telecom, transport, food, or health venture may need approval from NRB, NTA, DoTM, a local body, or another regulator. **5. Meet labour obligations** by using clear contracts, minimum wage, working-hour, leave, social-security, and safety rules. **6. Protect data** under the Individual Privacy Act 2075, with consent, limited collection, security, and breach procedures. **7. Keep records and filings** for accounting, tax, VAT, TDS, and corporate filings. The exact requirements depend on structure, sector, staff, and location.

Use written compliance checklists and assign an owner to every recurring obligation. Keep registration certificates, tax records, contracts, policies, and renewal dates in one accessible file. Review the setup whenever the product, staff count, location, or funding structure changes. Early professional advice is cheaper than correcting a misclassified worker, missing permit, ownership dispute, or data breach after launch.

*(204 words)*

### B5. Discuss the importance of ICT in developing a marketing strategy

ICT makes a marketing strategy measurable, fast, and precise. Digital channels let a startup reach narrow target segments instead of buying broad, untargeted media. Campaign data supports estimates of which channels drove visits, leads, installs, purchases, or repeat use. Analytics platforms combine this evidence into dashboards, while A/B testing compares messages before full rollout. A customer relationship management system connects campaigns to follow-up and retention. E-commerce and social platforms also permit rapid pricing, promotion, and inventory changes. ICT also supports localisation through Nepali and English content, mobile-first design, and local payment options. Its value is not simply collecting more data. The team must connect each objective to a metric such as cost per acquired customer, conversion rate, retention, or lifetime value, then act on the result. Without that discipline, digital tools only produce expensive reports and do not improve the strategy.

The strategy should also connect acquisition with retention and lifetime value. A campaign that produces cheap installs but frequent cancellations may destroy value. Use cohort data to see which customers renew, purchase again, or refer others. Local payment methods, relevant languages, and mobile-first design remove practical barriers in Nepal. The founder must set one objective, one responsible team, and one review cycle for every channel.

*(206 words)*

### B6. How can ICT be aligned with leadership characteristics?

ICT should support the leadership trait that matters for the current stage. **Opportunity recognition** improves when analytics expose changing customer behaviour and market signals. **Resilience** improves when teams retain experiment, customer-feedback, and incident data, then learn from failure without blame. **Resource orchestration** improves through shared dashboards, cloud tools, and communication platforms that connect specialists. **Vision and execution** improve when goals, priorities, owners, and deadlines remain visible in project and performance tools. **Servant leadership** improves when ICT removes routine work, supports asynchronous participation, and gives employees easier access to information and feedback. ICT can also weaken leadership if a founder becomes controlling, uses surveillance instead of trust, or creates a tool-heavy culture. The fit is therefore deliberate: choose software that improves the behaviour the venture needs, train leaders to interpret its outputs, and keep final decisions human and accountable.

**Example:** A ride-sharing startup needs faster incident response. It uses a shared dashboard to flag repeated ride cancellations, live incident chat to connect the operations lead with the driver and rider, and an incident log to track the cause and corrective action. The dashboard shows patterns, but the team reviews context before acting. Access to dashboards must match responsibility, and personal data should remain limited to what the team needs. Algorithms may support decisions, but humans remain accountable. Regular team conversations remain necessary because technology cannot reveal fear, conflict, or lack of skill.

*(231 words)*

### B7. Justify: an efficient CRM implementation leads to proper customer retention

The statement is justified because CRM gives a startup a continuous record of each customer's journey. It identifies buying patterns, preferences, complaints, usage, and signs that a customer may leave. This turns retention from guesswork into planned action. The system can trigger a welcome sequence after signup, renewal reminders before expiry, and a support response after a complaint. It also assigns ownership so no customer issue is lost between sales and support. Management can compare retention by channel, segment, product, and campaign, then remove activities that fail. For a Nepali startup, CRM can combine WhatsApp, email, app notifications, and support records, which fits customers who communicate across several channels. CRM does not retain customers by itself. Poor service, weak products, or careless data collection will still cause churn. It works when the company records reliable data, responds quickly, learns from complaints, and changes the customer experience. Therefore, an efficient CRM creates the visibility and timely action that make retention manageable.

It also supports prediction: repeated complaint or inactivity patterns reveal where the product fails. The strongest justification therefore combines data, timely action, and visible responsibility for customer outcomes.

*(199 words)*

## Group C — Long Answer Questions [attempt any THREE] [3 × 10 = 30]

### C1. How can different ICT tools be used for effective consumer insight? Explain with example.

Consumer insight means understanding what customers need, why they buy, what stops them, and how they behave. ICT tools gather and analyse evidence at low cost and high speed. **1. Surveys and forms.** Google Forms or a survey app can test price sensitivity, feature priorities, and awareness. Segment results by profession, age, location, and ride frequency rather than accepting one average answer. **2. Interviews and feedback.** Recorded interviews, support chats, and in-app feedback reveal the reason behind an answer. A team can tag recurring complaints without exposing personal data. **3. Web and app analytics.** Tools such as Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics, or Mixpanel show where users drop out, which features they repeat, and how acquisition channels convert. **4. Product tests and prototypes.** Figma clickable prototypes, usability sessions, A/B tests, and concierge MVPs show real behaviour before a full build. **5. Market and review mining.** Google Trends, keyword tools, and app-store reviews reveal rising needs and gaps left by competitors. **6. CRM and social listening.** CRM connects purchases and support history; social tools track complaints and conversations. **Example:** A ride-sharing startup tests a safety feature. Surveys show women value verified drivers and SOS functions. Session recordings show users cannot find the emergency button. App analytics show non-users leave before payment. The team then simplifies the feature, tests fare transparency, and measures repeat rides. ICT supplies evidence; interviews and observed behaviour explain why it matters.

The process should combine tools rather than rely on one dashboard. Begin with a decision: whether to build a safety feature, what price to test, or which customer segment to target. Select only the tool that can reduce that decision's uncertainty. Separate behavioural evidence from stated preference because users may praise a feature but fail to use it. Check sample quality, consent, privacy, and possible bias. Store each finding beside the assumption it tests, then repeat it after a product change. This creates a learning loop: collect evidence, interpret it with customers, change the offer, and measure behaviour again. In Nepal, language and connectivity differences matter, so include users outside the capital and test low-data mobile flows. The result is not more data. It is a faster, safer decision about the customer problem.

*(366 words)*

### C2. What are the key components of cash-flow management? How can IT tools support efficient cash-flow management?

Cash-flow management ensures that money enters and leaves the business at the right time. **1. Forecasting.** Prepare best, base, and worst-case inflows and outflows. **2. Cash collection.** Invoice promptly, use reliable digital payment methods, and follow up on overdue accounts. **3. Payment control.** Schedule payroll, suppliers, tax, and loans by due date and negotiate suitable terms. **4. Working-capital control.** Shorten the cash-conversion cycle by receiving cash quickly and paying suppliers within agreed terms. **5. Cash reserves and runway.** Keep an operating reserve and monitor months of cash left at the current burn. **6. Scenario and risk control.** Stress-test sales delays, customer concentration, and currency or regulatory changes. IT supports each step. Accounting software records receipts and bills; bank feeds update balances automatically; dashboards show cash by account and forecast date; accounting-integrated payment tools support collections; approval workflows control spending; inventory and billing systems reduce stock and receivable delays; low-code planning tools create scenarios; alerts flag overdue invoices, tax dates, and low runway. The entrepreneur must still interpret the output. A dashboard does not solve a weak business model; it exposes the warning early enough to act.

Management should also separate cash from profit and measure the gap. A startup can report a profit while a large unpaid invoice or delayed salary drains its bank account. A rolling cash forecast should therefore begin with expected receipts by date, not with accounting revenue. The founder should compare forecast with actual receipts weekly and investigate any repeated gap. Customer concentration deserves special attention: one late enterprise payer can threaten the whole runway even when total sales look healthy. The team should set collection targets, offer convenient digital payment, invoice milestones in advance, and stop work that becomes impossible to finance. Supplier terms should match the payment cycle rather than distort it. Tax and statutory payments should be ring-fenced when they become due, not treated as leftover cash. Finally, the forecast should include a weekly minimum-cash rule. If projected cash falls below that rule, the founder must cut spending, speed collections, arrange a short facility, or raise funds before a crisis. IT provides timely visibility; disciplined management provides the response.

*(356 words)*

### C3. Describe revenue-stream types and explain the tools and importance of cost-structure analysis

A **revenue stream** describes how a business earns money. Common types include **transaction revenue**, charged per sale, ride, or order; **subscription revenue**, paid at a fixed interval; **usage-based revenue**, linked to consumption; **advertising and sponsorship revenue**; **licensing or subscription software revenue**; **transaction or marketplace fees** from other users; and **data or insight revenue** where lawful and anonymised. A venture can combine several streams, but each needs a target customer, price, billing method, and margin test. **Cost-structure analysis** identifies fixed costs, such as core salaries and software, and variable costs, such as payment fees, cloud usage, incentives, and commissions. Tools include accounting software, bank feeds, inventory systems, payroll platforms, spreadsheets, and unit-economics dashboards. The analysis matters because revenue alone hides whether the business is sustainable. It reveals gross margin, customer acquisition cost, contribution per transaction, break-even volume, and cash needs. Pricing, product scope, supplier contracts, and funding decisions should then follow the evidence. A high-revenue model with poor margins can consume cash quickly; a lower-revenue model may still survive if costs scale carefully.

The analysis should connect every stream with the cost required to earn it. For one ride, subtract gateway charges, driver incentives, support time, payment collection, and mapped operating cost from the fare. For software, calculate hosting, support, development, and sales cost against recurring subscription revenue. For advertising, estimate the audience, sale rate, and revenue required to cover the campaign. This reveals whether a popular service creates value or merely creates work. A good dashboard can display revenue, direct cost, contribution margin, customer acquisition cost, payback period, and break-even volume by product and channel. It should also show trends and scenarios because one month's totals can hide seasonality or growing support load.

The result guides action. A weak margin may require a higher price, fewer discounts, a lower-cost delivery channel, or removal of an unprofitable product. A high acquisition cost may require better onboarding or stronger referrals. Fixed costs that no longer match the stage should be reduced carefully. Managers should review the analysis monthly and explain every major change. The core principle is simple: revenue is an input, but contribution and cash are the outcome. Cost-structure analysis shows whether each revenue stream deserves to stay and what the venture must change before it scales.

*(376 words)*

### C4. What legal challenges do Nepali IT startups face? Propose a comprehensive strategy.

Nepali IT startups face several connected challenges. **1. Ambiguous classification.** A ride-hailing, food-delivery, or freelance platform may fit several legal categories, while partner drivers can be treated as workers, contractors, or independent partners. **2. Licensing and permits.** Payment, telecom, transport, health, food, and import activities may need sector approval and local permissions. **3. Tax compliance.** Companies must manage corporate income tax, VAT, TDS, advance tax, and differing treatment of digital exports and small enterprises. **4. Employment and social security.** Written contracts, working hours, leave, gratuity, provident fund, and social-security rules create obligations when a venture grows. **5. Intellectual property.** Brand, code, design, and employee inventions may be unclear if ownership and confidentiality are not documented. **6. Data and cybersecurity.** Consent, purpose limitation, security, breach response, and cross-border data handling are essential for trust. **7. Consumer and platform liability.** Complaints, refunds, accident claims, fraud, and unfair contract terms may have no clear platform-specific rule. A comprehensive strategy should begin with a legal and regulatory map for the exact product, supported by current advice from IRD, labour lawyers, the relevant regulator, and the relevant police or transport authority. The startup should choose a clear structure, register and document properly, use written employment and contractor terms, maintain a permit and compliance calendar, protect IP and data, and use written policies for refunds, safety, and complaints. It should also keep evidence, train staff, review rules each quarter, and budget for professional advice before scaling.

The strategy should assign responsibility. A founder or compliance lead owns a dated legal register covering registrations, licences, tax filings, social security, contracts, insurance, and policy reviews. Before a launch or new feature, the team records the users, money flow, data collected, workers involved, and any regulated activity. This short assessment identifies which permits and contracts may be required. When the position is unclear, the startup should obtain a written opinion rather than publish an assumption. Staff need simple training on consent, records, complaints, and incident escalation. Customers need visible terms, refund rules, privacy information, and a support channel. Finally, the board or founder should review incidents, complaints, regulator notices, and near misses every quarter. Each event should produce a recorded corrective action with an owner and date. This turns legal work from a launch checklist into a cycle of prevention, detection, correction, and learning.

*(391 words)*

## Group D — Comprehensive Case Analysis [20]

### D1. Lead Founder of a technology-driven startup in Nepal

You are appointed as the Lead Founder of a new technology-driven startup in Nepal. The company must develop from an idea to sustainable operation.

#### (a) Idea Development Process and Target Market

First, define the problem through field observation, user interviews, and analysis of existing workarounds. Separate the customer segment from the technology segment. For example, teachers, small clinics, and delivery firms may need different products even if the same backend is used. Scan market, technology, regulatory, and social trends. Apply creative-thinking tools such as brainstorming, SCAMPER, and design thinking to generate several concepts. Screen each idea against desirability, feasibility, and viability. Build a low-fidelity prototype and run a concierge or MVP test. Measure activation, task completion, repeat use, and willingness to pay.

Next, define the initial target market using four filters: demographics, geography, behaviour or usage, and the problem or need. Avoid "everyone in Nepal." Select one narrow beachhead, such as small medical clinics in Kathmandu that lack a simple appointment and payment system. State the buyer, user, payer, and influencer separately because they may differ. Estimate TAM, SAM, and SOM from primary data rather than a headline population figure. Validate the beachhead through a measurable pilot. The first decision is not which technology to build; it is which customer problem is frequent, urgent, and worth paying to solve.

#### (b) Digital Marketing to Acquire Customers

Begin with the target segment's channels and message. For clinics, this could include search ads for appointment software, Facebook or Instagram education, referral programmes, and partnerships with medical associations. Use a landing page to explain the problem and offer a demo or free pilot. Track each step: visit, lead, qualified trial, activation, paid conversion, and repeat use. Give every campaign a target cost per acquired customer and a conversion target. Test one message at a time rather than changing the whole campaign.

Build trust before asking for payment. Show data-security practices, customer support, transparent pricing, and a real customer story. Use a referral loop only after the product delivers value. Keep existing users engaged through onboarding messages and useful health content; retention creates referrals and lowers acquisition cost. Review performance weekly and stop weak channels. Digital marketing works when it connects a clear customer problem to measurable behaviour, not when it merely increases impressions.

#### (c) Three Funding Sources, Budgeting, and Liquidity

Use three sources for different needs. **Bootstrapping** from founder savings, customer revenue, or early service income preserves control and proves demand, but it limits speed. **Angels or accelerators** can provide early capital, mentorship, and contacts in exchange for equity and founder commitments. **Grants or concessional funds** can support research, training, or equipment when the venture meets the scheme's conditions, although they are slower and restricted. Debt should follow predictable revenue rather than precede it.

Prepare a zero-based budget from the product roadmap. Separate fixed costs, variable costs, one-off launch costs, and a contingency reserve. Attach every cost to a milestone or measurable output. Build a rolling twelve-month cash forecast with best, base, and worst cases. Match customer collections and supplier payments to due dates. Keep operating, payroll, tax, and reserve cash separate. Set a minimum runway and raise funds before the agreed warning level. Review actual costs against the budget monthly. Liquidity survives when the founder knows the date of the next cash shortage, controls spending, and preserves a buffer before revenue becomes predictable.

#### (d) Leadership Style and Conflict Management

The most effective style is usually **situational leadership**, supported by a clear vision and firm safety or ethics boundaries. A new team may need direction because systems and skills are weak. As competence grows, shift towards coaching and participation. For a mature, committed team, delegate outcomes with clear decision rights. Autocratic control should remain only for emergencies, safety, ethics, or cash-critical decisions.

Handle conflict through a defined process. Pause when emotions are high. Separate facts from assumptions, and interests from personal positions. Let each party explain its concern, ask what information is missing, and generate several options. Decide against objective criteria such as customer safety, evidence, cost, and agreed company values. Record the decision, owner, deadline, and review date. Use a founder agreement, role ownership, regular team meetings, and blameless post-mortems to prevent hidden conflict from returning. This combination gives the startup direction without creating a culture where people avoid speaking.

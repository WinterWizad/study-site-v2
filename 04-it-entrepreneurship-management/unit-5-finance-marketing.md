## 5.1 Funding sources for startups (A7, C18)

> **A7 asks for any two. C18 asks for any three, explained.** Memorise the full menu, pick the three you can explain best.

| Source | Mechanism | Pros | Cons | Nepal example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrapping** | founder savings, revenue reinvestment | full control, no dilution | slow growth, personal risk | most Nepali startups (e.g., SastoDeal early years) |
| **Friends & Family** | personal network investment | fast, flexible terms | relationship risk, limited amount | common first round |
| **Angel Investors** | HNIs invest own money for equity | mentorship, network, follow-on | dilution, valuation pressure | One Ladder, Business Oxygen, individual angels |
| **Venture Capital (VC)** | funds invest pooled money for equity | large capital, scaling support, credibility | high dilution, control loss, exit pressure | few formal VCs in Nepal (Dolma, True North) |
| **Grants / Competitions** | non-dilutive cash from govt/NGO/uni | free money, credibility | small amounts, reporting burden | Antarprerana, World Bank, UNDP, university grants |
| **Bank Loans** | debt with interest, collateral | no dilution, tax-deductible interest | repayment obligation, collateral needed | limited for early startups (no assets) |
| **Revenue-based Financing** | % of monthly revenue until cap repaid | no equity, flexible repayment | higher cost than debt, needs revenue | emerging in Nepal (e.g., via fintech platforms) |
| **Crowdfunding** | small amounts from many people | market validation, community | platform fees, all-or-nothing risk | limited in Nepal (no dedicated platform) |
| **Strategic / Corporate Investment** | corporate invests for strategic fit | distribution, credibility, resources | strategic alignment required | e.g., telecom investing in fintech |
| **Government Schemes** | subsidised loans, equity funds | low cost, policy support | bureaucracy, conditions | Youth Self-Employment Fund, SME loans |

### A7 · "List any two funding sources for startups." [2]

> Two funding sources: (1) **Bootstrapping** — founder savings and revenue reinvestment; full control, no dilution, but slow growth. (2) **Angel Investors** — high-net-worth individuals invest own money for equity; provide mentorship and network, but cause dilution and valuation pressure.

*(54 words. Two names + mechanism + one pro/con each.)*

### C18 · "What are the different sources of funding for startups? Explain any three." [10]

> **Shape: three sources, each with mechanism + pros/cons + Nepal example. ~400 words.**

Three major funding sources for startups:

**1. Bootstrapping** — the founder uses personal savings and reinvests revenue. *Mechanism:* zero external capital; growth funded by operations. *Pros:* full ownership and control; no dilution; no investor pressure. *Cons:* slow growth; personal financial risk; limited scale. *Nepal example:* **SastoDeal** bootstrapped for years, reinvesting marketplace commissions before taking investment.

**2. Angel Investors** — high-net-worth individuals invest their own money for equity. *Mechanism:* typically NPR 50L–2Cr for 5–15% stake; often provide mentorship and network. *Pros:* smart capital; faster than VC; flexible terms. *Cons:* dilution; valuation negotiation; limited follow-on capacity. *Nepal example:* **One Ladder Ventures**, **Business Oxygen**, and individual angels like Binod Chaudhary have backed early-stage Nepali tech startups.

**3. Venture Capital (VC)** — professional funds invest pooled money for equity. *Mechanism:* Series A/B/C rounds; NPR 2Cr–50Cr+ for 10–30% stake; board seat, liquidation preferences. *Pros:* large capital for scaling; credibility; follow-on reserves; network. *Cons:* high dilution; loss of control; exit pressure (5–7 years); high growth expectations. *Nepal example:* **Dolma Impact Fund**, **True North Associates** — few formal VCs in Nepal; most "VC" rounds are angel syndicates.

## 5.2 Budgeting techniques for new ventures

| Technique | Description | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Zero-based budgeting** | every expense justified from zero each period | cost discipline, early stage |
| **Rolling forecast** | update forecast monthly/quarterly for next 12 months | volatile environment, startup |
| **Top-down + Bottom-up** | targets from leadership + detail from teams | alignment + realism |
| **Milestone-based budgeting** | funds released on hitting milestones | investor-backed, phased execution |
| **Contingency buffer** | 10–20% of budget for unknowns | all startups (uncertainty is high) |

## 5.3 Cash flow management essentials

| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Cash is king** — profit ≠ cash; a profitable startup can die from cash gap | track weekly |
| **Shorten cash conversion cycle** — collect fast (prepaid, auto-charge), pay slow (negotiate terms) | rider prepaid wallet, driver weekly payout |
| **Maintain runway** — months of cash at current burn; target 12–18 months | raise before 6 months left |
| **Separate accounts** — operating, tax, payroll, reserve | prevents accidental spend |
| **Scenario planning** — base, best, worst case cash flows | update monthly |

## 5.6 Digital marketing techniques (A8, C20b)

### A8 · "What is digital marketing?" [2]

> Digital marketing is the promotion of products/services using digital channels — **search (SEO/SEM), social media (FB/Instagram/LinkedIn), email, content, influencer, affiliate, and mobile (SMS/app push)** — to reach, engage, and convert target audiences, with measurable ROI through analytics.

*(52 words. Definition + seven named channels + measurable ROI.)*

### C20b (half) · "Explain the digital marketing and sales techniques." [5]

> **Shape: digital marketing (4 channels) + sales (3 techniques) + Nepal example. ~200 words.**

**Digital marketing techniques:**

1. **Search (SEO/SEM)** — optimise for "ride sharing Kathmandu", "taxi app Nepal"; run Google Ads on high-intent keywords.
2. **Social media (Meta/Instagram)** — safety-first content, driver stories, referral campaigns, UGC (rider photos), targeted ads (professionals 25–40 in Kathmandu).
3. **Referral / Viral** — "Give NPR 100, Get NPR 100" — leverages network effects; low CAC, high trust.
4. **Content / Email / Push** — onboarding sequence, ride receipts, safety tips, reactivation campaigns for dormant users.

**Sales techniques:**

1. **B2B / Corporate sales** — pitch "employee commute solution" to HR/Finance of Kathmandu companies; monthly billing, dashboard, safety compliance.
2. **Driver acquisition** — on-ground camps, referral bonus (driver refers driver), low-commission launch period, vehicle financing partnerships.
3. **Partnership / Channel sales** — integrate with hotel/airline booking, event ticketing, corporate travel desks.

*Nepal example:* **Pathao** used referral virality + Facebook safety content + corporate commute pitches to dominate Kathmandu.

*(208 words)*

## 5.7 Sales techniques for startups

| Technique | When it works | Nepal ride-share example |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder-led sales** | early stage, high-touch, complex B2B | founder pitches corporate commute to HR |
| **Inbound / Product-led** | self-serve, low friction | rider downloads app, books instantly |

| **Referral / Viral** | network effects, low CAC | "Give 100, Get 100" rider referral |
| **Outbound / Cold** | high-value targets, known decision-makers | sales team calls corporate travel managers |

## 5.8 Customer Relationship Management (CRM) (B16)

> **CRM is the system and process for managing all interactions with current and potential customers — across acquisition, retention, and expansion — to maximise lifetime value.**

### B16 · "What is the role of CRM in startups?" [5]

> **Shape: definition → three roles → Nepal example. ~200 words.**

CRM (Customer Relationship Management) is the system and process for managing all interactions with current and potential customers across acquisition, retention, and expansion to maximise lifetime value.

**Three roles in a startup:**

1. **Acquisition tracking** — lead source, funnel stage, conversion rate, CAC by channel. *Nepal example:* track which channel (FB ad, referral, corporate) brought a rider; double down on lowest CAC.
2. **Retention & engagement** — ride frequency, churn risk, NPS, reactivation campaigns. *Nepal example:* dormant rider (30 days no ride) gets "We miss you" push with NPR 50 credit.
3. **Expansion & feedback** — upsell (rider pass), cross-sell (food delivery), NPS surveys, feature requests. *Nepal example:* rider with 20+ rides/month offered monthly pass at 15% discount; NPS survey after 10th ride.

**CRM tools for Nepali startups:** HubSpot (free tier), Zoho, Freshsales, or custom (Google Sheets → Airtable → HubSpot as scale grows).

*(208 words)*

## 5.9 Brand building essentials

| Element | What to decide |
|---|---|
| **Brand purpose** | why we exist beyond profit (e.g., "safe mobility for all") |
| **Brand positioning** | "safety-first ride-sharing" vs "most affordable" |
| **Visual identity** | logo, colour (safety blue), typography, iconography |
| **Voice & tone** | caring, clear, confident (not corporate) |
| **Consistency** | same voice/visuals across app, web, social, support, driver kit |
| **Community** | rider community (safety tips), driver community (earnings tips) |

## 5.10 Case study: MakeMyTrip's growth (reference)

> **MakeMyTrip (India)** — started as B2B travel agent (2000), pivoted to B2C online booking (2005), built **SEO dominance** for "flight booking India", **content marketing** (travel guides), **mobile-first** (app 60%+ bookings), **loyalty programme** (MMT Black), **corporate travel** (Quest2Travel). **Key lesson:** channel mastery (SEO → mobile → corporate) + brand trust (cancellation protection, 24/7 support) = market leadership.

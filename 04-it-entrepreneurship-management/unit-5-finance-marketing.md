## 5.1 Funding sources for startups (A7, C18)

> **A7 asks for any two. C18 asks for any three, explained.** Memorise the full menu, pick the three you can explain best.
>
> **Plain-language terms:** **dilution** means giving up part of the company's ownership; **non-dilutive funding** means receiving money without giving up ownership; **valuation** is the price investors assign to the company.

| Source | Mechanism | Pros | Cons | Nepal example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrapping** | founder savings, revenue reinvestment | full control, no dilution | slow growth, personal risk | ⚠ unverified: a founder interview described a self-funded period |
| **Friends & Family** | personal network investment | fast, flexible terms | relationship risk, limited amount | common first round |

### A7 · "List any two funding sources for startups." [2] 🎯 2-mark

> Two funding sources: (1) **Bootstrapping** — founder savings and revenue reinvestment; full control, no dilution, but slow growth. (2) **Angel Investors** — high-net-worth individuals invest own money for equity; provide mentorship and network, but cause dilution and valuation pressure.

*(54 words. Two names + mechanism + one pro/con each.)*

### C18 · "What are the different sources of funding for startups? Explain any three." [10] 🎯 10-mark

> **Shape: three sources, each with mechanism + pros/cons + Nepal example. ~400 words.**

Three major funding sources for startups:

**1. Bootstrapping** — the founder uses personal savings and reinvests operating revenue. *Mechanism:* customers fund growth, so the founder needs no external investor. *Pros:* full ownership, no dilution, and freedom to test the market quietly. *Cons:* the founder carries personal risk, growth is slow, and cash limits product development. *Nepal link:* SastoDeal publicly described a self-funded period before its first institutional round. The safe lesson is not the exact years or amounts; it is that revenue can validate demand before outside capital arrives.

**2. Angel investment** — one or more individuals invest personal capital in an early-stage company, usually for equity. *Mechanism:* the angel receives a stake or another agreed return and may join later rounds. *Pros:* faster than a full VC process, and angels often add industry knowledge, introductions, and mentorship. *Cons:* founders surrender some ownership, and one angel may not provide enough capital for a large scale-up. *Nepal link:* individual angels can enter through NIC, Antarprerana, and accelerator networks. Do not list a fund as an angel: One Ladder Ventures and Business Oxygen are investment funds, not individual angels.

**3. Venture capital** — a licensed fund invests money pooled from investors in exchange for equity and governance rights. *Mechanism:* the fund invests after checking the team, market, product, traction, and legal position, then negotiates valuation, ownership, and exit terms. *Pros:* it can provide a large round, specialist hiring support, follow-on reserves, and credibility. *Cons:* founders face dilution, may lose decision rights, and face pressure to grow quickly and find an exit. *Nepal link:* Dolma Impact Fund and TrueNorth are examples in the local early-stage landscape. The market remains small, so founders should compare the actual terms rather than chase a famous fund name.

**Selection rule:** use bootstrapping to prove demand, angels to accelerate a validated venture, and VC when the product has repeatable economics and needs a larger round. Match the source to the milestone. Never accept money before stating the amount, use of funds, expected runway, and rights each investor receives.

*(395 words)*

## 5.2 Budgeting techniques for new ventures

| Technique | Description | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Zero-based budgeting** | every expense justified from zero each period | cost discipline, early stage |
| **Rolling forecast** | update forecast monthly/quarterly for the next 12 months | volatile environment, startup |
| **Top-down + Bottom-up** | targets from leadership + detail from teams | alignment + realism |
| **Milestone-based budgeting** | funds released on hitting milestones | investor-backed, phased execution |
| **Contingency buffer** | reserve sized from identified risks and uncertainty | use when assumptions are volatile |

## 5.3 Cash flow management essentials

| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Cash matters more than paper profit** — profit ≠ cash; a profitable startup can still fail from a cash gap | track weekly |
| **Shorten the cash-conversion cycle** — collect customer cash quickly and pay suppliers within agreed terms | rider prepaid wallet, driver weekly payout |
| **Maintain runway** — the number of months the business can operate at its current spending rate; target 12–18 months | raise funds before only 6 months remain |
| **Separate accounts** — operating, tax, payroll, and reserve money | prevents accidental spending |
| **Scenario planning** — forecast cash flow under base, best, and worst conditions | update monthly |

### Board C2 · "Key components of cash-flow management and IT tools" [10] 🎯 10-mark

> Cash-flow management ensures that money enters and leaves the business at the right time. **1. Forecasting.** Prepare best, base, and worst-case inflows and outflows. **2. Cash collection.** Invoice promptly, use reliable digital payment methods, and follow up on overdue accounts. **3. Payment control.** Schedule payroll, suppliers, tax, and loans by due date and negotiate suitable terms. **4. Working-capital control.** Shorten the cash-conversion cycle by receiving cash quickly and paying suppliers within agreed terms. **5. Cash reserves and runway.** Keep an operating reserve and monitor months of cash left at the current burn. **6. Scenario and risk control.** Stress-test sales delays, customer concentration, and currency or regulatory changes. IT supports each step. Accounting software records receipts and bills; bank feeds update balances automatically; dashboards show cash by account and forecast date; accounting-integrated payment tools support collections; approval workflows control spending; inventory and billing systems reduce stock and receivable delays; low-code planning tools create scenarios; alerts flag overdue invoices, tax dates, and low runway. The entrepreneur must still interpret the output. A dashboard does not solve a weak business model; it exposes the warning early enough to act.
>
> Management should also separate cash from profit and measure the gap. A startup can report a profit while a large unpaid invoice or delayed salary drains its bank account. A rolling cash forecast should therefore begin with expected receipts by date, not with accounting revenue. The founder should compare forecast with actual receipts weekly and investigate any repeated gap. Customer concentration deserves special attention: one late enterprise payer can threaten the whole runway even when total sales look healthy. The team should set collection targets, offer convenient digital payment, invoice milestones in advance, and stop work that becomes impossible to finance. Supplier terms should match the payment cycle rather than distort it. Tax and statutory payments should be ring-fenced when they become due, not treated as leftover cash. Finally, the forecast should include a weekly minimum-cash rule. If projected cash falls below that rule, the founder must cut spending, speed collections, arrange a short facility, or raise funds before a crisis. IT provides timely visibility; disciplined management provides the response.

*(356 words)*

## 5.5 Developing a marketing strategy

| Component | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | "safe, affordable, transparent" vs "premium" vs "cheapest" |
| **Target segments** | primary: Kathmandu professionals; secondary: students, tourists |
| **Value proposition** | upfront fare, safety features, 24/7 support, loyalty |
| **Channels** | digital (FB/Google/Instagram), referral, corporate, PR, offline events |
| **Funnel metrics** | awareness → install → first ride → repeat → advocate |
| **Budget allocation** | % of revenue; test → scale winners; kill losers |
| **Brand building** | consistent visual/voice; safety-first narrative; community |

### Board B5 · "Importance of ICT in developing a marketing strategy" [5] 🎯 5-mark

> ICT makes a marketing strategy measurable, fast, and precise. Digital channels let a startup reach narrow target segments instead of buying broad, untargeted media. Campaign data supports estimates of which channels drove visits, leads, installs, purchases, or repeat use. Analytics platforms combine this evidence into dashboards, while A/B testing compares messages before full rollout. A customer relationship management system connects campaigns to follow-up and retention. E-commerce and social platforms also permit rapid pricing, promotion, and inventory changes. ICT also supports localisation through Nepali and English content, mobile-first design, and local payment options. Its value is not simply collecting more data. The team must connect each objective to a metric such as cost per acquired customer, conversion rate, retention, or lifetime value, then act on the result. Without that discipline, digital tools only produce expensive reports and do not improve the strategy.
>
> The strategy should also connect acquisition with retention and lifetime value. A campaign that produces cheap installs but frequent cancellations may destroy value. Use cohort data to see which customers renew, purchase again, or refer others. Local payment methods, relevant languages, and mobile-first design remove practical barriers in Nepal. The founder must set one objective, one responsible team, and one review cycle for every channel.

*(206 words)*

## 5.6 Digital marketing techniques (A8, C20b)

### A8 · "What is digital marketing?" [2] 🎯 2-mark

**Plain-language terms:** **SEO** means search engine optimisation (improving organic search results); **SEM** means search engine marketing (paid search ads); **ROI** means return on investment; **FB** is Facebook.

> Digital marketing promotes products or services through digital channels such as search engines, social media, email, content, mobile apps, and online advertising. It targets a defined audience, engages users, and drives measurable actions such as leads, purchases, or app installs. Analytics track channel performance and guide budget decisions, although not every conversion can be attributed with certainty.

*(52 words. Definition + seven named channels + measurable ROI.)*

### C20b (half) · "Explain the digital marketing and sales techniques." [5] 🎯 5-mark

> **Shape: digital marketing (4 channels) + sales (3 techniques) + Nepal example. ~200 words.**

**Digital marketing techniques:**

1. **Search (SEO/SEM)** — optimise for "ride sharing Kathmandu", "taxi app Nepal"; run Google Ads on high-intent keywords.
2. **Social media (Meta/Instagram)** — safety-first content, driver stories, referral campaigns, UGC (rider photos), targeted ads (professionals 25–40 in Kathmandu).
3. **Referral / Viral** — "Give NPR 100, Get NPR 100" — leverages network effects; low CAC, high trust.
4. **Content / Email / Push** — onboarding sequence, ride receipts, safety tips, reactivation campaigns for dormant users.

**Sales techniques:**

1. **B2B / Corporate sales** — pitch "employee commute solution" to HR/Finance of Kathmandu companies; monthly billing, dashboard, safety compliance.
2. **Driver acquisition** — on-ground camps, referral bonus (driver refers driver), low-commission launch period, vehicle financing partnerships.
3. **Partnership / Channel sales** — integrate with hotel/airline booking, event ticketing, corporate travel desks.

*Nepal illustration:* Pathao publicly launched a "Give NPR 100, Get NPR 100" rider referral in 2017. It shows how a two-sided offer can reduce first-use friction, but the team must still test repeat use and cost per acquired rider.

*(208 words)*

## 5.8 Customer Relationship Management (CRM) (B16)

**Plain-language terms:** **CAC** means customer acquisition cost; **LTV** means customer lifetime value; **NPS** means net promoter score (a satisfaction metric); **churn** means customers who stop using the service.

> **CRM is the system and process for managing all interactions with current and potential customers — across acquisition, retention, and expansion — to maximise lifetime value.**

### B16 · "What is the role of CRM in startups?" [5] 🎯 5-mark

> **Shape: definition → three roles → Nepal example. ~200 words.**

CRM (Customer Relationship Management) is the system and process for managing all interactions with current and potential customers across acquisition, retention, and expansion to maximise lifetime value.

**Three roles in a startup:**

1. **Acquisition tracking** — lead source, funnel stage, conversion rate, CAC by channel. *Nepal example:* track which channel (FB ad, referral, corporate) brought a rider; double down on the most cost-effective channel.
2. **Retention and engagement** — ride frequency, churn risk, satisfaction scores, reactivation campaigns. *Nepal example:* an inactive rider receives a relevant reactivation message with an appropriate offer.
3. **Expansion and feedback** — upsell, cross-sell, satisfaction surveys, feature requests. *Nepal example:* a frequent rider is offered a monthly pass after the team confirms that usage makes it useful.

**CRM tools for Nepali startups:** HubSpot (free tier), Zoho, Freshsales, or custom (Google Sheets → Airtable → HubSpot as scale grows).

*(207 words)*

### Board A10 · "Mention any two importance of CRM" [2] 🎯 2-mark

> First, CRM helps a startup understand prospects. It records leads, activities, and conversion data, so founders can stop spending on weak channels. Second, it supports retention by tracking usage, complaints, satisfaction, and renewal signals. The team can contact inactive customers before they leave, improving lifetime value and revenue predictability.

*(51 words)*

### Board B7 · "Justify: efficient CRM leads to proper customer retention" [5] 🎯 5-mark

> The statement is justified because CRM gives a startup a continuous record of each customer's journey. It identifies buying patterns, preferences, complaints, usage, and signs that a customer may leave. This turns retention from guesswork into planned action. The system can trigger a welcome sequence after signup, renewal reminders before expiry, and a support response after a complaint. It also assigns ownership so no customer issue is lost between sales and support. Management can compare retention by channel, segment, product, and campaign, then remove activities that fail. For a Nepali startup, CRM can combine WhatsApp, email, app notifications, and support records, which fits customers who communicate across several channels. CRM does not retain customers by itself. Poor service, weak products, or careless data collection will still cause churn. It works when the company records reliable data, responds quickly, learns from complaints, and changes the customer experience. Therefore, an efficient CRM creates the visibility and timely action that make retention manageable.
>
> It also supports prediction: repeated complaint or inactivity patterns reveal where the product fails. The strongest justification therefore combines data, timely action, and visible responsibility for customer outcomes.

*(199 words)*

It combines a distinctive name and visual design with consistent communication, service quality, and customer experience. A startup defines its audience and positioning, delivers a reliable promise, listens to feedback, and uses suitable channels. Strong brands lower acquisition cost and improve retention.

## 5.10 Case study: MakeMyTrip's growth — an analysis

> **Scope:** This case uses MakeMyTrip's public company history, FY23 reporting, and public web-traffic estimates. It does not claim that one tactic alone caused its growth.

| Strategic move | What MakeMyTrip did | Why it helped growth | Limit or risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Changed the business model** | Began in 2000 as a B2B travel agency and moved into consumer online booking in 2005 | Put customers directly into the booking flow, created transaction data, and shifted the firm from selling agency access to operating a travel platform | A wider platform creates heavier support, refund, and supplier-management demands |
| **Won high-intent search demand** | Built destination guides, booking content, and useful route or hotel information for search engines | Travellers often search before they buy. Useful content brought relevant visitors, improved organic reach, and reduced dependence on paid ads | Ranking alone cannot convert. Offers, inventory, trust, and checkout still determine the sale |
| **Moved mobile-first** | Built a mobile booking journey and used app notifications, stored travel details, and repeat search | Mobile removed the desktop detour and supported frequent, low-friction bookings | App acquisition can be expensive. Discounting and install campaigns may hide weak retention |
| **Reduced trust risk** | Combined price visibility, reviews, payment choices, booking confirmation, support, and cancellation/refund processes | Travel buyers fear hidden costs and non-refundable mistakes. Clear information and recovery reduce the perceived cost of trying the service | Bad experiences spread quickly through reviews and social media. Support must match the promise |
| **Built repeat and business channels** | Added loyalty offers, corporate travel products, and partnerships that supplied inventory or distribution | Repeat buyers lower acquisition cost. Corporate and partner channels create steadier demand and new customer pools | Discounting, partner dependence, and corporate sales cycles can weaken margins and control |

**Evidence check:** The cited FY23 report and Similarweb estimate are not saved in this workspace, so this note uses no quantitative app-booking or traffic-share figures. Do not memorise numerical figures; use the qualitative finding: mobile and search were important channels, but scale alone does not prove which tactic caused growth.

**Exam conclusion:** MakeMyTrip grew by combining a consumer platform model, search-led discovery, mobile convenience, trust, and repeat-purchase channels. Each element solved a different customer barrier. A Nepali startup should copy the sequence — remove one barrier, measure behaviour, and add the next channel only when unit economics work. It should not copy discounts or spend without checking retention and contribution margin.

## 6.1 Leadership Styles in Entrepreneurship (A9, C19) 🎯 2-mark / 🎯 10-mark

### A9 · "Define leadership style." [2] 🎯 2-mark

> Leadership style is the consistent pattern of behaviour a leader uses to influence, guide, and direct a team — encompassing decision-making approach, communication mode, delegation level, and motivation method. It ranges from **autocratic** (leader decides) to **democratic** (team decides) to **laissez-faire** (team self-directs), with **transformational** (inspire change) and **servant** (serve the team) as modern variants.

*(52 words. Definition + spectrum + five named styles.)*

---

### C19 · "Explain different leadership styles used in startups." [10] 🎯 10-mark

> **Shape: 4–5 styles, each with definition + when it works + startup context. ~400 words.**

**Five leadership styles for startups:**

1. **Autocratic** — leader decides unilaterally; top-down commands. *When it works:* crisis, early-stage chaos, regulatory compliance, time-critical decisions. *Startup context:* founder sets non-negotiables (safety standards, legal compliance, cash discipline) in Year 1.

2. **Democratic (Participative)** — leader involves team in decisions; consensus-seeking. *When it works:* complex problems needing diverse input, building buy-in, culture formation. *Startup context:* product roadmap prioritisation, hiring decisions, culture values definition.

3. **Transformational** — leader inspires with vision, challenges assumptions, develops people. *When it works:* scaling phase, culture change, attracting talent, pivot moments. *Startup context:* articulating "why we exist" (e.g., "safe mobility for all"), rallying team through funding winter.

4. **Servant Leadership** — leader serves the team; removes obstacles, empowers, grows people. *When it works:* knowledge-worker teams, high autonomy, strong culture. *Startup context:* engineering/product teams; leader clears blockers, advocates for resources, celebrates team wins.

5. **Situational / Adaptive** — leader flexes style based on team maturity and task urgency (Hersey-Blanchard). *When it works:* always — the meta-skill for founders. *Startup context:* autocratic for safety/cash; democratic for product; transformational for vision; servant for team growth.

**The startup reality:** no single style works forever. Founders start autocratic (survival), shift democratic (culture), become transformational (scale), and practise servant daily (team). The best founders are **situational** — they diagnose the moment and choose.

*(404 words)*

## 6.2 Building Effective Teams

| Principle | What it means | Startup application |
|---|---|---|
| **Hire for values, train for skills** | cultural fit > technical perfection | define 3–4 core values; interview for them |
| **Diverse perspectives** | cognitive diversity > demographic checkboxes | mix of domains (tech, ops, business), backgrounds |
| **Clear roles + shared goals** | RACI matrix; OKRs aligned to vision | every hire knows their "one metric" |
| **Psychological safety** | speak up without fear; fail fast, learn fast | blameless post-mortems; leader admits mistakes first |

## 6.3 Conflict Resolution Techniques (B17) 🎯 5-mark

> **Conflict in startups is inevitable — co-founder disputes, priority clashes, resource allocation, equity splits. The skill is resolving it fast without relationship damage.**

---

### B17 · "Explain the conflict resolution techniques within the team." [5] 🎯 5-mark

> **Shape: 5 techniques, one-line each + when to use. ~200 words.**

Five conflict resolution techniques for startup teams:

1. **Collaborating (Win-Win)** — both parties work together to find a solution meeting both needs. *Use when:* relationship matters, issue is complex, time allows. *Example:* co-founders disagree on product direction → workshop to integrate both visions.

2. **Compromising (Split the Difference)** — each gives up something for a middle ground. *Use when:* time pressure, equal power, temporary fix. *Example:* marketing vs product on launch date → launch MVP now, full features in v1.1.

3. **Accommodating (Yield)** — one yields to preserve relationship. *Use when:* issue matters more to other party, relationship > outcome. *Example:* designer insists on UI detail; PM yields to build trust.

4. **Competing (Assert)** — one pushes own view, leverages authority. *Use when:* emergency, safety, ethics, non-negotiable. *Example:* founder overrides team on safety feature before launch.

5. **Avoiding (Defer)** — postpone or sidestep. *Use when:* trivial issue, emotions high, more info needed. *Example:* heated debate on office snacks → "let's decide next week."

**The startup rule:** default to **collaborating**; use **competing** only for safety/ethics/cash; **avoid** avoiding for strategic issues.

*(208 words)*

## 6.4 Motivating Employees in Startups (C20a) 🎯 5-mark

> **C20 is a split question: "How do you motivate employees in startups? Explain the digital marketing and sales techniques." Answer in two headed halves.**

---

### C20a · "How do you motivate employees in startups?" [5] 🎯 5-mark

> **Shape: 4 drivers of motivation + startup context + Nepal example. ~200 words.**

Motivation in startups comes from four drivers, not just salary:

1. **Purpose & Vision** — people work harder when they believe in the "why". *Action:* articulate a compelling mission ("safe mobility for all"); connect daily work to impact. *Nepal example:* Pathao drivers motivated by "income with dignity" narrative.

2. **Autonomy & Ownership** — give people control over *how* they achieve outcomes. *Action:* set clear outcomes (OKRs), let teams choose methods; avoid micromanagement. *Nepal example:* engineering team chooses tech stack within architecture guardrails.

3. **Mastery & Growth** — people want to get better at what they do. *Action:* learning budget, mentorship, stretch projects, visible career path. *Nepal example:* quarterly "learning Friday"; sponsor AWS/Azure certs; internal tech talks.

4. **Recognition & Fair Rewards** — timely, specific recognition; equity/profit-share aligned with contribution. *Action:* public shoutouts, spot bonuses, transparent ESOP vesting, performance-linked bonus. *Nepal example:* monthly "Driver of the Month" (rider ratings + safety); ESOP pool 10% for all employees.

**The startup reality:** cash is tight; purpose, autonomy, mastery, and recognition are the currency you *can* afford.

*(204 words)*

## 6.5 Networking Skills for Entrepreneurs

| Skill | What it means | Startup application |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic mapping** | identify who you need (investors, mentors, partners, talent) | maintain a "top 50" list; review monthly |
| **Value-first approach** | give before you ask; share insights, intros, resources | "How can I help?" before "Can you invest?" |
| **Warm introductions** | leverage mutual connections; cold outreach < 10% success | ask portfolio founders for VC intros |
| **Personal brand** | consistent voice on LinkedIn/Twitter; share lessons, not wins | "What I learned raising our seed round" |
| **Follow-up discipline** | 24-hour thank you; monthly check-in; CRM for network | HubSpot/Airtable for "top 50" contacts |

## 6.6 Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) (A10) 🎯 2-mark

### A10 · "What do you mean by corporate social responsibility (CSR)?" [2] 🎯 2-mark

> CSR is a company's commitment to operate ethically and contribute to sustainable development — balancing profit with positive impact on society, environment, and stakeholders. It goes beyond compliance to **voluntary actions** addressing community, ecology, labour rights, and governance. For startups, CSR is not "charity after profit" but **building responsibility into the business model from day one**.

*(54 words. Definition + "beyond compliance" + "voluntary" + "business model integration".)*

## 6.8 Ethical Marketing Strategies (C21a) 🎯 5-mark

> **C21 is a split question: "Discuss about ethical marketing strategies. What are the essential elements of a business plan?" Answer in two headed halves.**

---

### C21a · "Discuss about ethical marketing strategies." [5] 🎯 5-mark

> **Shape: 4 principles + Nepal ride-share example. ~200 words.**

Ethical marketing means promoting truthfully, respecting user autonomy, and avoiding manipulation — building trust as a long-term asset.

**Four principles:**

1. **Truthfulness & Transparency** — no false claims, hidden fees, or bait-and-switch. *Startup:* upfront fare, no surge surprise, clear cancellation policy.
2. **Respect for Privacy** — consent-based data use; no dark patterns; easy opt-out. *Startup:* rider data used only for ride matching; no sale to third parties; clear privacy policy.
3. **Inclusivity & Non-Discrimination** — marketing reflects diverse users; no stereotypes. *Startup:* women riders featured; accessibility features highlighted; language options (Nepali/English).
4. **Social Responsibility** — marketing supports positive behaviour; no exploitation of vulnerability. *Startup:* promote helmet use, safe riding, driver welfare; no "drink and ride" campaigns.

**The business case:** ethical marketing builds **brand trust** — the only defensible moat in a commoditised market. Unethical marketing wins short-term, loses long-term.

*(204 words)*

## 6.9 Case Study: Tata Group's CSR Initiatives (reference)

> **Tata Group (India)** — CSR embedded since 1868. **Key initiatives:** Tata Trusts (66% of Tata Sons equity funds philanthropy), **Tata Steel** (tribal education, healthcare, water in Jharkhand/Odisha), **TCS** (adult literacy, STEM for girls), **Tata Power** (rural electrification, solar), **Tata Chemicals** (sustainable agriculture, water conservation). **Governance:** CSR committee at board level; 2%+ of avg net profit spent; impact measured, not just spend. **Key lesson:** CSR is **governance, not charity** — integrated into strategy, measured, and led from the top.

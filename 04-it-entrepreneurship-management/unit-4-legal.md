## 4.1 Nepali business laws for startups — the key statutes

### Legal and business terms in plain English

| Term | Plain meaning | Why a startup cares |
|---|---|---|
| **Pvt Ltd** | private limited company | chooses a legal form and brings company and filing duties |
| **PAN** | Permanent Account Number | identifies the taxpayer to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) |
| **CIT** | corporate income tax | tax paid on a company's profit |
| **VAT** | value-added tax | tax on taxable sales; registration and filing may apply |
| **TDS** | tax deducted at source | the payer withholds tax before making the payment |
| **Advance tax** | part of estimated annual tax paid during the year | reserves cash before the year-end bill |
| **SST / SSF** | social-security contributions managed through the Social Security Fund | affects payroll, enrolment, and records |
| **PF** | Provident Fund contribution | part of the employee's retirement savings |
| **Gratuity** | service-linked payment after qualifying service | an employment cost when the rule applies |
| **DPO** | data protection officer | privacy role where the current rules require one |
| **OCR** | Office of Company Registrar | handles company registration and company filings |
| **DoTM** | Department of Transport Management | handles transport-related permissions and enforcement |

| Statute | What it governs | Relevance to IT startups |
|---|---|---|
| **Companies Act, 2063 (2006)** | company registration, governance, compliance, and winding up (closing the company) | Pvt Ltd registration, director duties, AGM, filings |
| **Electronic Transactions Act, 2063 (2006)** | digital signatures, electronic records, cybercrime, and intermediary liability (responsibility of online intermediaries) | app terms of service, data protection, e-contracts |
| **Income Tax Act, 2058 (2002)** | corporate tax, TDS, VAT, advance tax, incentives | tax registration, filing, deductions, startup incentives |
| **Labour Act, 2074 (2017)** | employment contracts, wages, hours, leave, termination, social security | driver/employee contracts, gratuity, PF, health insurance |
| **Intellectual Property laws** (see §4.2) | patents, trademarks, copyrights, designs | code, brand, logo, algorithm protection |
| **Foreign Investment & Technology Transfer Act, 2075 (2019)** | FDI approval, repatriation (moving profit abroad), technology transfer | if raising foreign capital |
| **Data Privacy** — **Individual Privacy Act, 2075 (2018)** | personal-data collection, processing, rights, and breach notification | user data, rider/driver information, consent, DPO |

## 4.2 Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) (A6)

> **IPR are legal rights over inventions, brands, code, and designs.** They give creators control over commercial use for a set period.

### The 4 main types (memorise for A6)

| Type | What it protects | Duration (Nepal) | IT startup example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patent** | inventions (new, inventive, industrially applicable) | 20 years from filing | novel algorithm, encryption method, hardware device |
| **Trademark** | brand identifiers (name, logo, slogan, sound) | 10 years, renewable indefinitely | app name, logo, tagline "Ride Safe" |
| **Copyright** | original literary, artistic, musical works (including code) | life of author + 50 years | source code, UI design, database, marketing content |
| **Industrial Design** | aesthetic appearance of a product | 5 years initial, renewable twice for 5 years each (max 15 years) | unique shape of a hardware IoT device |

### A6 · "What is intellectual property right (IPR)?" [2] 🎯 2-mark

> IPR are legal rights protecting creations of the mind, giving creators exclusive control over their inventions, brands, code, and designs. Four main types: (1) **Patent** — novel inventions (algorithm, hardware), 20 years. (2) **Trademark** — brand identifiers (name, logo, slogan), 10 years renewable. (3) **Copyright** — original works including source code, UI, content, life + 50 years. (4) **Industrial Design** — aesthetic appearance of a product, 5 years initial, renewable twice for 5 years each (max 15 years).

## 4.4 Taxation basics for IT startups in Nepal (B15)

> **Nepal's tax regime for IT startups has specific incentives and compliance requirements.**

### The 5 tax heads (memorise for B15)

| Tax | Rate / Rule | Key points for IT startups |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate Income Tax (CIT)** | 25% standard; **20% for full-year, notified IT-industry income** (Income Tax Act §11(2ख)) | file annually by the end of Ashad; advance tax: 40% by Poush-end, 70% by Chaitra-end, 100% by Ashad-end (Income Tax Act §94) |
| **Value Added Tax (VAT)** | 13% | registration required if preceding 12-month turnover > NPR 50 lakh **or** taxable transactions > NPR 30 lakh; file within 30 days of crossing threshold; monthly returns; claim valid input credit on business expenses |
| **Tax Deducted at Source (TDS)** | varies by payment type, recipient, VAT status, and threshold | §88: 15% on specified payments to non-resident/non-VAT-registered; §88(1)(4): 1.5% on service fees to VAT-registered/exempt; §89(1): 1.5% on contracts > NPR 50,000; §89(1): 10% on rent; deposit by 25th of following month |
| **Advance Tax** | cumulative instalments of estimated tax | 40% by Poush-end, 70% by Chaitra-end, 100% by Ashad-end (Income Tax Act §94) |
| **Social Security Tax (SST)** | 20% (11% employer + 9% employee) | applies to covered formal employees; contributions go to the Social Security Fund (SSF) |

### IT-specific incentives (Income Tax Act, 2058)

| Incentive | Condition |
|---|---|
| **20% CIT** (vs 25% standard) | full-year operation and income from government-notified "information-technology industry" (Income Tax Act §11(2ख)) |
| **5-year tax holiday (SEZ/IT Parks only)** | available only in a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) or IT Park — verify the current Finance Act |
| **Accelerated depreciation** | computers/data-processing equipment depreciable at 25%; first-year fraction follows 3/2/1-month rule (Income Tax Act Schedule 2) |
| **R&D deduction** | qualifying R&D expenditure deductible up to 50% of taxable business income (Income Tax Act §18) |

### B15 · "Explain taxation basics for IT startups in Nepal." [5] 🎯 5-mark

> **Shape: five tax heads with rate/rule + IT incentives. ~200 words.**

Nepal's taxation for IT startups covers five heads:

1. **Corporate Income Tax (CIT)** — 25% standard; **20% for full-year, notified IT-industry income** (Income Tax Act §11(2ख)). File annually by Ashad end; advance tax: 40% by Poush-end, 70% by Chaitra-end, 100% by Ashad-end (Income Tax Act §94).
2. **Value Added Tax (VAT)** — 13%. Registration required if preceding 12-month turnover > NPR 50 lakh **or** taxable transactions > NPR 30 lakh; file within 30 days of crossing threshold; monthly returns; input credit on business expenses.
3. **Tax Deducted at Source (TDS)** — varies by payment type, recipient, VAT status, and threshold: §88: 15% on specified payments to non-resident/non-VAT-registered; §88(1)(4): 1.5% on service fees to VAT-registered/exempt; §89(1): 1.5% on contracts > NPR 50,000; §89(1): 10% on rent; deposit by 25th of following month.
4. **Advance Tax** — cumulative instalments of estimated tax: 40% by Poush-end, 70% by Chaitra-end, 100% by Ashad-end (Income Tax Act §94).
5. **Social Security Tax (SST)** — 20% total (11% employer + 9% employee). Applies to covered formal employees; deposited to Social Security Fund.

**IT-specific incentives:** (1) **20% CIT** for full-year, notified IT-industry income. (2) **5-year tax holiday (SEZ/IT Parks only)** — verify current Finance Act. Also: accelerated depreciation (25% depreciable, first-year fraction 3/2/1 months) and R&D deduction (qualifying spend up to 50% of taxable income).

*(208 words)*

## 4.5 Compliance with Labour Laws

| Requirement | Labour Act, 2074 provision | Startup application |
|---|---|---|
| **Employment contract** | written, in Nepali or English, before joining | use clear terms for employees and separately review partner agreements |
| **Minimum wage** | notified annually | check the current rate and apply it to the relevant worker |
| **Working hours** | 8 hours/day, 48 hours/week; overtime at 1.5× | track driver shifts and office-staff hours |
| **Leave** | 18 days annual, 12 days sick, 98 days maternity | record leave as it accrues for employees |
| **Gratuity** | 8.33% of basic salary per year | builds up as service is completed; apply only when the rule applies |
| **Provident Fund (PF)** | 10% employer + 10% employee | retirement savings for covered employees; deposited monthly |
| **Health insurance** | mandatory group policy | covers employees when the rule applies |
| **Termination** | notice (30 days) or compensation; valid grounds | record the reason and follow the correct process for performance, redundancy, or misconduct |
| **Social Security Fund (SSF)** | 20% (11% + 9%) under the social-security scheme | enrol covered formal employees and keep contribution records |

**Gig workers (drivers) vs employees:** the Act applies to workers. Calling a driver a “partner” does not settle the legal classification. If the actual working relationship is employment, employment and social-security duties can apply. The position can change, so obtain current labour advice before launch.

## 4.6 Case sub-part (D22d) — "Complexities due to policy and law" [5]

> **Shape: 5 categories of legal/policy complexity for a ride-sharing app. ~150 words.**

Policy and law complexities for a ride-sharing app in Nepal:

1. **Company & transport regulation** — Pvt Ltd registration (OCR); transport-service name registration under Motor Vehicles Act §93; no separate "platform" category in the Act.
2. **Labour classification** — drivers as "partners" vs "employees" — Labour Act 2074 applies to workers; if the actual relationship is employment, employment and social-security duties can apply. Obtain current labour advice.
3. **Data privacy** — Individual Privacy Act 2075: consent for rider/driver data, purpose limitation, data minimisation. ⚠ The Act does not require controller registration, a DPO, a 72-hour breach notice, or cross-border transfer rules.
4. **Tax compliance** — CIT: 25% standard, 20% for full-year notified IT-industry income; VAT: registration if turnover > 50 lakh or taxable transactions > 30 lakh; TDS on driver payouts per specific sections and thresholds; advance tax: 40% Poush, 70% Chaitra, 100% Ashad; SST on driver earnings if classified as workers.
5. **Consumer protection & safety** — no dedicated ride-sharing regulation; liability for accidents unclear; insurance mandate: third-party (NPR 50 lakh) and passenger cover (NPR 1 lakh death >5 yr, NPR 50K ≤5 yr, NPR 25K <1 yr); personnel accident cover for drivers (NPR 5 lakh); surge pricing transparency; dispute resolution mechanism.

*(169 words)*

## 4.7 Board-paper answers

### Board A4 · "What is intellectual property right?" [2] 🎯 2-mark

> Intellectual property rights are legal rights protecting creations of the mind. They give owners control over commercial use and protection from unauthorised copying. Nepal's main categories are patents for inventions, trademarks for brand names and logos, copyright for original works and software code, and industrial designs for product appearance (5 years initial, renewable twice for 5 years each, max 15 years). Startups should register important assets and protect confidential know-how through contracts.

*(60 words)*

### Board A8 · "Define labour law" [2] 🎯 2-mark

> Labour law governs the relationship between employers and workers. It covers recruitment, contracts, wages, working hours, leave, workplace safety, social security, termination, and dispute resolution. In Nepal, the Labour Act 2074 is the main statute, supported by social-security rules. A startup must classify platform workers correctly and document terms fairly, because app-based work can create legal uncertainty.

*(57 words)*

### Board B4 · "Describe basic legal requirements for startups in Nepal" [5] 🎯 5-mark

> A Nepali startup should complete a basic legal and compliance setup. **1. Register the business** with the Office of Company Registrar or use the legally appropriate registration route. **2. Obtain tax registration** with the Inland Revenue Department, including PAN and VAT registration when turnover or taxable-transaction thresholds require it. **3. Protect intellectual property** through patent, trademark, copyright, or design registration and written employee and contractor agreements. **4. Check sector permission.** A fintech, payment, telecom, transport, food, or health venture may need approval from NRB, NTA, DoTM, a local body, or another regulator. **5. Meet labour obligations** by using clear contracts, minimum wage, working-hour, leave, social-security, and safety rules. **6. Protect data** under the Individual Privacy Act 2075, with consent, limited collection, security, and breach procedures. **7. Keep records and filings** for accounting, tax, VAT, TDS, and corporate filings. The exact requirements depend on structure, sector, staff, and location.

Use written compliance checklists and assign an owner to every recurring obligation. Keep registration certificates, tax records, contracts, policies, and renewal dates in one accessible file. Review the setup whenever the product, staff count, location, or funding structure changes. Early professional advice is cheaper than correcting a misclassified worker, missing permit, ownership dispute, or data breach after launch.

*(204 words)*

### Board C4 · "What are the legal challenges faced by Nepali startups in IT sector? As an IT manager propose a comprehensive strategy to address the legal challenges in IT sector." [10] 🎯 10-mark

> Nepali IT startups face several connected challenges. **1. Ambiguous classification.** A ride-hailing, food-delivery, or freelance platform may fit several legal categories, while partner drivers can be treated as workers, contractors, or independent partners. **2. Licensing and permits.** Payment, telecom, transport, health, food, and import activities may need sector approval and local permissions. **3. Tax compliance.** Companies must manage CIT (25% standard, 20% for full-year notified IT-industry income), VAT (registration if turnover > 50 lakh or taxable transactions > 30 lakh), TDS per specific sections and thresholds, advance tax (40/70/100 cumulative schedule), and differing treatment of digital exports and small enterprises. **4. Employment and social security.** Written contracts, working hours, leave, gratuity, provident fund, and social-security rules create obligations when a venture grows. **5. Intellectual property.** Brand, code, design, and employee inventions may be unclear if ownership and confidentiality are not documented. **6. Data and cybersecurity.** Consent, purpose limitation, security, breach response. ⚠ The Privacy Act 2075 does not require a DPO, a 72-hour breach notice, or cross-border transfer rules. **7. Consumer and platform liability.** Complaints, refunds, accident claims, fraud, and unfair contract terms may have no clear platform-specific rule. A comprehensive strategy should begin with a legal and regulatory map for the exact product, supported by current advice from IRD, labour lawyers, the relevant regulator, and the relevant police or transport authority. The startup should choose a clear structure, register and document properly, use written employment and contractor terms, maintain a permit and compliance calendar, protect IP and data, and use written policies for refunds, safety, and complaints. It should also keep evidence, train staff, review rules each quarter, and budget for professional advice before scaling.

The strategy should assign responsibility. A founder or compliance lead owns a dated legal register covering registrations, licences, tax filings, social security, contracts, insurance, and policy reviews. Before a launch or new feature, the team records the users, money flow, data collected, workers involved, and any regulated activity. This short assessment identifies which permits and contracts may be required. When the position is unclear, the startup should obtain a written opinion rather than publish an assumption. Staff need simple training on consent, records, complaints, and incident escalation. Customers need visible terms, refund rules, privacy information, and a support channel. Finally, the board or founder should review incidents, complaints, regulator notices, and near misses every quarter. Each event should produce a recorded corrective action with an owner and date. This turns legal work from a launch checklist into a cycle of prevention, detection, correction, and learning.

*(391 words)*

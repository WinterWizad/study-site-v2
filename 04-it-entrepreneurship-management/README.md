## The three things that decide this paper

1. **Unit 2 and Unit 6 are the mark engines** — 29 marks each. They are also the most "conceptual" — students treat them as common sense and write vague answers. Structure every answer: definition → numbered points → Nepal example.
2. **Units 3 and 4 are the easy marks** — definitions, lists, Nepal law. Memorise BMC 9 blocks, business plan 10 elements, IPR types, tax heads, labour law sections. Zero creativity needed.
3. **The case is 20 marks and follows a fixed skeleton** — idea generation (Unit 2) → market analysis (Unit 2) → rider trust (Unit 5/4) → policy/law (Unit 4). Learn the four-part skeleton, not Pathao facts.

## Study order for this subject

The mark distribution dictates the study order:

1. **Unit 2 (Idea Generation & Market Research)** — 29 marks, 10 hours → highest yield
2. **Unit 6 (Leadership & CSR)** — 29 marks, 6 hours → second engine
3. **Unit 5 (Finance & Marketing)** — 24 marks, 10 hours → funding, digital marketing, CRM
4. **Unit 1 (Foundations)** — 9 marks, 6 hours → quick definitions
5. **Unit 3 (Business Model/Plan)** — 12 marks, 10 hours → BMC, plan elements
6. **Unit 4 (Legal/Regulatory)** — 12 marks, 6 hours → Nepal law, tax, IPR, labour

**Do not study in syllabus order.** Study in marks-per-hour order.

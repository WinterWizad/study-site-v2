## 1.1 What entrepreneurship is (A1)

> **Entrepreneurship means finding an opportunity, gathering resources, and creating value while accepting risk and uncertainty.**
>
> The venture may be new or part of an existing organisation. The key test is a useful opportunity, not simply self-employment.

Three clauses carry that definition:

| Clause | What it means | Why the examiner cares |
|---|---|---|
| **identifying an opportunity** | not just "starting a business" — it starts with a problem worth solving | separates entrepreneurship from self-employment |
| **assembling resources** | people, capital, technology, partnerships — the founder rarely owns all of them | the "resource orchestration" view |
| **creating value** | economic (revenue, jobs), social (inclusion, access), or both | value creation, not just activity |

### Why entrepreneurship matters

| Importance | What it changes | Nepal link |
|---|---|---|
| **Creates jobs and income** | turns an idea into an operating venture that hires and buys services | supports youth employment without relying only on migration |
| **Solves real problems** | converts unused resources or ignored needs into useful products and services | better access to payments, education, health, and public services |
| **Drives innovation** | tests new technology, processes, and delivery models | supports the country's digital-economy transition |
| **Uses resources productively** | organises capital, skills, land, and knowledge under risk and uncertainty | helps redeploy entrepreneurship talent inside Nepal |
| **Builds businesses and ecosystems** | creates companies, suppliers, networks, and new funding markets | strengthens the wider startup and innovation ecosystem |

**Exam line:** Entrepreneurship matters because it converts opportunities into value by creating jobs, solving problems, using resources productively, and building the businesses and support networks around them.

### A1 · "Define entrepreneurship." [2] 🎯 2-mark

> Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying an opportunity, assembling resources (people, capital, technology), and creating value through a new or existing venture while accepting risk and uncertainty. It is not merely self-employment — it begins with a problem worth solving and creates economic or social value.

*(52 words. Definition + three clauses + distinction from self-employment.)*

## 1.3 Key characteristics of successful entrepreneurs (B11)

> **Shape: name three → explain each with one sentence → one Nepal example. ~200 words.**

The research (Kuratko, Kawasaki, field studies) consistently finds **five core traits**. Pick any three, but these three are the most examinable:

| # | Characteristic | What it means in practice | Nepal example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opportunity recognition** | sees a problem others ignore and frames it as a business opportunity | Amit Agrawal (eSewa) saw cash-dominance as a payments opportunity, not a barrier |
| 2 | **Resilience / grit** | persists through failure, rejection, cash crunches; treats failure as data | Pathao founders survived 2015 blockade, regulation, and funding winters |
| 3 | **Resource orchestration** | uses outside money, skills, technology, or partnerships to achieve more | Khalti used existing telecom and bank networks instead of owning them |
| 4 | **Risk management** | takes calculated risks, not blind leaps; uses MVP, lean testing | Tootle launched as MVP on Facebook before building the app |
| 5 | **Vision with execution** | articulates a compelling future AND delivers daily progress | SastoDeal combined marketplace vision with daily logistics execution |

### B11 · "Explain any three key characteristics of successful entrepreneurs." [5] 🎯 5-mark

> Three key characteristics:
>
> 1. **Opportunity recognition** — sees a problem others ignore and frames it as a business opportunity. *Example:* eSewa's founders saw cash-dominance as a payments opportunity, not a barrier.
> 2. **Resilience / grit** — persists through failure, rejection, and cash crunches; treats failure as data. *Example:* Pathao survived the 2015 blockade, regulatory uncertainty, and funding winters.
> 3. **Resource orchestration** — gets more done with less by leveraging partnerships and bootstrapping. *Example:* Khalti built on existing telecom and bank infrastructure instead of owning it.
>
> (Also examinable: risk management — calculated risks via MVP; vision with execution — articulates future and delivers daily.)

## 1.4 Entrepreneurship ecosystem (A2)

> **An entrepreneurship ecosystem is the interconnected set of actors, institutions, and conditions that enable or constrain new venture creation and growth in a given geography.**

### Six pillars (memorise this list for A2)

| Pillar | What it includes | Nepal status (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Policy & regulation** | company registration, tax incentives, bankruptcy law, IP protection | improving: Company Act 2063, Startup Policy (draft), but enforcement gaps |
| **2. Finance** | angel networks, VC, bank lending, grants, crowdfunding | nascent: few formal VCs (e.g., One Ladder, Business Oxygen), mostly grants/bootstrapping |
| **3. Human capital** | talent pool, universities, immigration, mentors | growing: CS/engineering grads, but brain drain; mentorship via accelerators (Next Launchpad, Antarprerana) |
| **4. Markets** | early adopters, corporate procurement, export access | small domestic; export via digital services (Upwork, Fiverr) and IT outsourcing |
| **5. Culture** | risk tolerance, failure acceptance, role models | shifting: failure still stigmatised; success stories (eSewa, Pathao, SastoDeal) changing perception |
| **6. Support infrastructure** | incubators, accelerators, co-working, legal/accounting services | Kathmandu-centric; expanding to Pokhara, Butwal, Biratnagar |

### A2 · "What is an entrepreneurship ecosystem?" [2] 🎯 2-mark

> An entrepreneurship ecosystem is the interconnected set of actors, institutions, and conditions enabling new venture creation in a geography. Six pillars: (1) **Policy & regulation** — registration, tax, IP (Nepal: Company Act 2063, draft Startup Policy). (2) **Finance** — angels, VC, grants (Nepal: nascent, mostly bootstrapping/grants). (3) **Human capital** — talent, mentors (Nepal: grads growing, brain drain, accelerators). (4) **Markets** — early adopters, corporate buyers (Nepal: small domestic, digital export). (5) **Culture** — risk tolerance, role models (Nepal: shifting, eSewa/Pathao as models). (6) **Support infrastructure** — incubators, co-working (Nepal: Kathmandu-centric).

*(56 words. Definition + six named pillars with one-line Nepal context each.)*

## 1.5 Role of ICT in entrepreneurship

> **ICT means information and communication technology.** It includes computers, mobile phones, the internet, software, and digital payment systems. It is not just a sector — it lowers the barriers to entry for many types of venture.

| How ICT enables | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Lowers startup cost** | cloud hosting (AWS/GCP) avoids buying servers; SaaS (software as a service) replaces tools built from scratch |
| **Expands market reach** | digital marketing, e-commerce, remote sales — global from day one |
| **Enables new models** | platforms, marketplaces, SaaS, fintech — impossible without ICT |
| **Accelerates learning** | analytics, A/B testing, user feedback loops — faster product-market fit |
| **Facilitates funding** | crowdfunding, digital pitch decks, virtual data rooms, online due diligence |

**Nepal illustration:** eSewa and Khalti are ICT ventures *and* ICT enablers. Their merchant and payment networks helped thousands of Nepali businesses accept digital payments without buying a separate payment system.

## 1.6 Case study: eSewa / Khalti and their fintech impact

> **eSewa (2009)** — Nepal's first licensed digital wallet. It began with utility-bill payments, then expanded into merchant payments, bank transfers, e-commerce, and government use through connectIPS. **Key lesson:** solve a frequent daily pain first, then expand.
>
> **Khalti (January 2017)** — entered a crowded market through **user experience** (UX: how easy and pleasant the app feels) and **merchant distribution** (helping small shops accept QR payments). Its electronic **know-your-customer (e-KYC)** checks made sign-up faster. **Key lesson:** improve the customer experience and reach, not only being first.
>
> **Impact on Nepal's fintech industry:** These wallets helped normalise digital payment for households and small merchants. They created reusable acceptance infrastructure for delivery, education, travel, and e-commerce businesses. They also produced transaction records that support accounting, credit assessment, and fraud controls. The shift is incomplete: NRB's *Payment Systems Report 2023* recorded mobile-money accounts at roughly 1.2 million while cash still represented about 78% of transaction value. **Exam conclusion:** eSewa and Khalti changed both customer behaviour and the fintech market, but Nepal still needs wider acceptance, financial inclusion, and reliable digital infrastructure.

### Board A1 · "Define any two types of entrepreneurship." [2] 🎯 2-mark

> **Innovative entrepreneurship** introduces a new product, service, process, or model and grows by solving a problem differently. It accepts higher uncertainty because the solution is untested. **Social entrepreneurship** uses a sustainable business model to solve an environmental or social problem while earning enough revenue to continue. Its main test is measurable social impact alongside financial sustainability.

### Board A6 · "What is a business ecosystem?" [2] 🎯 2-mark

> A business ecosystem is the connected network of companies, customers, investors, institutions, infrastructure, and rules supporting activity in a market. Each participant depends on the others, so one weak link can limit the group. A digital-payment ecosystem needs users, merchants, banks, operators, regulators, internet access, and dispute handling. Nepal needs stronger finance and predictable rules for smaller businesses.

*(58 words)*

### Board B1 · "Discuss the role of ICT for effective entrepreneurship." [5] 🎯 5-mark

> ICT supports entrepreneurship in five linked ways. **First, it lowers entry cost.** Cloud hosting, open-source software, shared workspaces, and digital payment tools replace large upfront investments in servers, offices, and equipment. **Second, it expands reach.** A small team can sell software or services to customers across Nepal and overseas. **Third, it enables new business models.** Platforms, marketplaces, fintech, SaaS, and digital delivery depend on data networks and software. **Fourth, it speeds learning.** Analytics, online feedback, A/B tests, and rapid prototypes reveal customer preferences before heavy spending. **Fifth, it improves coordination and finance.** Digital accounting, CRM, remote collaboration, and crowdfunding connect founders with staff, customers, and capital. ICT cannot replace product-market fit, sound regulation, or capable people, but it lets a lean startup learn and scale much faster.
>
> ICT also changes the founder's role. Instead of supervising every task, the leader can manage data, experiment, and relationships. A small team can test several ideas at once and keep the best. The limitation is important: digital access is uneven, cyber risk rises, and platforms can depend on a few large suppliers. ICT helps most when the venture pairs it with reliable infrastructure, security, digital literacy, and clear rules for handling customer data.

*(200 words)*

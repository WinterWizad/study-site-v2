## 1.1 What Entrepreneurship Is (A1) 🎯 2-mark

> **Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying an opportunity, assembling the resources to pursue it, and creating value — through a new venture or within an existing organisation — while accepting the inherent risk and uncertainty.**

Three clauses carry that definition:

| Clause | What it means | Why the examiner cares |
|---|---|---|
| **identifying an opportunity** | not just "starting a business" — it starts with a problem worth solving | separates entrepreneurship from self-employment |
| **assembling resources** | people, capital, technology, partnerships — the founder rarely owns all of them | the "resource orchestration" view |
| **creating value** | economic (revenue, jobs), social (inclusion, access), or both | value creation, not just activity |

---

### A1 · "Define entrepreneurship." [2] 🎯 2-mark

> Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying an opportunity, assembling resources (people, capital, technology), and creating value through a new or existing venture while accepting risk and uncertainty. It is not merely self-employment — it begins with a problem worth solving and creates economic or social value.

*(52 words. Definition + three clauses + distinction from self-employment.)*

## 1.3 Key Characteristics of Successful Entrepreneurs (B11) 🎯 5-mark

> **Shape: name three → explain each with one sentence → one Nepal example. ~200 words.**

The research (Kuratko, Kawasaki, field studies) consistently finds **five core traits**. Pick any three, but these three are the most examinable:

| # | Characteristic | What it means in practice | Nepal example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opportunity recognition** | sees a problem others ignore and frames it as a business opportunity | Amit Agrawal (eSewa) saw cash-dominance as a payments opportunity, not a barrier |
| 2 | **Resilience / grit** | persists through failure, rejection, cash crunches; treats failure as data | Pathao founders survived 2015 blockade, regulation, and funding winters |
| 3 | **Resource orchestration** | gets more done with less — leverages partnerships, bootstraps, attracts talent | Khalti built on existing telecom/bank infrastructure instead of owning it |
| 4 | **Risk management** | takes calculated risks, not blind leaps; uses MVP, lean testing | Tootle launched as MVP on Facebook before building the app |
| 5 | **Vision with execution** | articulates a compelling future AND delivers daily progress | SastoDeal combined marketplace vision with daily logistics execution |

---

### B11 · "Explain any three key characteristics of successful entrepreneurs." [5] 🎯 5-mark

> Three key characteristics:
>
> 1. **Opportunity recognition** — sees a problem others ignore and frames it as a business opportunity. *Example:* eSewa's founders saw cash-dominance as a payments opportunity, not a barrier.
> 2. **Resilience / grit** — persists through failure, rejection, and cash crunches; treats failure as data. *Example:* Pathao survived the 2015 blockade, regulatory uncertainty, and funding winters.
> 3. **Resource orchestration** — gets more done with less by leveraging partnerships and bootstrapping. *Example:* Khalti built on existing telecom and bank infrastructure instead of owning it.
>
> (Also examinable: risk management — calculated risks via MVP; vision with execution — articulates future and delivers daily.)

*(204 words)*

## 1.4 Entrepreneurship Ecosystem (A2) 🎯 2-mark

> **An entrepreneurship ecosystem is the interconnected set of actors, institutions, and conditions that enable or constrain new venture creation and growth in a given geography.**

### Six Pillars (memorise this list for A2)

| Pillar | What it includes | Nepal status (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Policy & regulation** | company registration, tax incentives, bankruptcy law, IP protection | improving: Company Act 2063, Startup Policy (draft), but enforcement gaps |
| **2. Finance** | angel networks, VC, bank lending, grants, crowdfunding | nascent: few formal VCs (e.g., One Ladder, Business Oxygen), mostly grants/bootstrapping |
| **3. Human capital** | talent pool, universities, immigration, mentors | growing: CS/engineering grads, but brain drain; mentorship via accelerators (Next Launchpad, Antarprerana) |
| **4. Markets** | early adopters, corporate procurement, export access | small domestic; export via digital services (Upwork, Fiverr) and IT outsourcing |
| **5. Culture** | risk tolerance, failure acceptance, role models | shifting: failure still stigmatised; success stories (eSewa, Pathao, SastoDeal) changing perception |
| **6. Support infrastructure** | incubators, accelerators, co-working, legal/accounting services | Kathmandu-centric; expanding to Pokhara, Butwal, Biratnagar |

---

### A2 · "What is an entrepreneurship ecosystem?" [2] 🎯 2-mark

> An entrepreneurship ecosystem is the interconnected set of actors, institutions, and conditions enabling new venture creation in a geography. Six pillars: (1) **Policy & regulation** — registration, tax, IP (Nepal: Company Act 2063, draft Startup Policy). (2) **Finance** — angels, VC, grants (Nepal: nascent, mostly bootstrapping/grants). (3) **Human capital** — talent, mentors (Nepal: grads growing, brain drain, accelerators). (4) **Markets** — early adopters, corporate buyers (Nepal: small domestic, digital export). (5) **Culture** — risk tolerance, role models (Nepal: shifting, eSewa/Pathao as models). (6) **Support infrastructure** — incubators, co-working (Nepal: Kathmandu-centric).

*(56 words. Definition + six named pillars with one-line Nepal context each.)*

## 1.5 Role of ICT in Entrepreneurship

> **ICT is not just a sector — it is an enabler that lowers the barriers to entry for every type of venture.**

| How ICT enables | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Lowers startup cost** | cloud (AWS/GCP) replaces server capex; SaaS replaces custom software |
| **Expands market reach** | digital marketing, e-commerce, remote sales — global from day one |
| **Enables new models** | platforms, marketplaces, SaaS, fintech — impossible without ICT |
| **Accelerates learning** | analytics, A/B testing, user feedback loops — faster product-market fit |
| **Facilitates funding** | crowdfunding, digital pitch decks, virtual data rooms, online due diligence |

**Nepal illustration:** eSewa and Khalti are ICT ventures *and* ICT enablers — they built digital payments infrastructure that 10,000+ other Nepali startups now use to accept payments.

## 1.6 Case Study: eSewa / Khalti (One Paragraph for Context)

> **eSewa (2009)** — Nepal's first digital wallet. Started as a utility bill payment app, expanded to merchant payments, bank transfers, and became the de facto payment rail for e-commerce (Daraz, SastoDeal) and government (connectIPS integration). **Key lesson:** solved a *daily pain* (queues for bill payment) first, then expanded.
>
> **Khalti (2017)** — entered a "crowded" market by focusing on **user experience** (instant KYC, clean UI) and **merchant acquisition** (QR codes for small shops). **Key lesson:** differentiation via UX and distribution, not just being first.

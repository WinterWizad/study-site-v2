# Study Loop (daily, 3–4 hrs)

The notes are only half the job. Marks come from recall under time pressure.

## Block structure (one subject per day, never mixed)

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Read | 40–60 min | one unit file, no highlighting. Close it. |
| 2. Blurt | 10 min | blank page: write everything you remember about that unit |
| 3. Gap check | 10 min | reopen the file, mark what you missed in red |
| 4. Quiz | 20 min | I run the unit's self-test + 5 rapid-fire 1-markers |
| 5. Write | 30 min | one 3-mark and one 5-mark answer, timed, by hand |
| 6. Mark | 10 min | I mark against the model answer and the marks-length rule |

Repeat for a second unit if time allows. Block 5 is the one students skip — it is the one that converts reading into marks.

## Escalation as the exam nears

- **Week 1:** 2 units/day, blurt + quiz only.
- **Week 2:** full Group A+B+C drills in 45 minutes, then Group D essays.
- **Final 48 h:** cheat sheets only, no new material, one timed paper.

## Rules
- Never re-read a unit you have already quizzed at ≥80% — spend the time on a weaker one.
- Any answer I mark below 60% goes on the repair list; the repair list is the only thing I re-teach.
- Group D (20 marks) is rehearsed at least three times per subject. It is one third of the paper.

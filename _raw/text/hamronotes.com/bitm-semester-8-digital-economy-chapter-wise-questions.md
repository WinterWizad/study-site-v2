Analyze the following case carefully and answer the questions that follow:

Agro-tech Concern is a web application launched in 2080 BS by three agricultural graduates and two computer programmers in Chitwan. The founders developed the platform: DB Rai, RB Bhandari, and SB Basnet, graduated from the Agriculture and Forestry University, Nepal, and developers, KB Shakya and TB Chhetri, graduated from Tribhuvan University, after observing how small farmers in the different reions of Nepal were constantly exploited by middlemen and languished in low productivity due to a lack of information. A vegetable farmer in Dhading, who was farming vegetables, could not know that the price in the Kathmandu market at Kalimati was triple the price he was getting in the local market. One of the rice growers in Ilam had no idea about the weather because they could not get any forecasts to determine when to plant or when to collect. Agro-tech Concern focused to fill these information gaps with a simple mobile application that could be accessed even on a basic smartphone.

The site provided four basic services. The first service was a weather advisory service, which gave a local seven-day weather forecast and seasonal recommendations on planting, which was based on the information supplied by the Department of Hydrology and Meteorology. The second service was a real-time market price dashboard, which consolidated wholesale and retail prices of 30 agricultural markets in Nepal, such as Kalimati, Balkhu, and big district market centers. The farmers had the power to negotiate better prices because they could check the existing prices in advance, before they were sold to traders. The third feature was a direct marketplace where farmers could post their produce and find buyers - restaurants, hotels, cooperatives, and urban consumers - without having to use the traditional wholesale intermediaries.The fourth was a peer-to-peer knowledge sharing group where farmers could post questions and get answers to their questions from agricultural extension officers, university researchers, and other experienced farmers in other districts.

The effects on the agricultural economy of Nepal were significant and unequal. Agro-tech concern has initiated well in the districts such as Kavre, Tanahun, and Jhapa, where the level of smartphone penetration was comparatively high, and the farmers were younger and more digitally comfortable. In two years, 85,555 farmers had been registered on the platform, and the marketplace feature had brought about direct transactions worth NPR 489 million. Farmers who used the price dashboard were reported to have made an average of 16 per cent above the price they used to get per harvesting cycle, compared to those who still used local traders to get price information. A local success story was a tomato farmer in Nuwakot, Rita Magar, when she sold her produce directly to a hotel chain in Hetauda via Agro-tech Concern at a price of NPR 66 per kilogram, as compared to the NPR 38 that the local middleman had been offering.

Nevertheless, the agricultural sector of Nepal was still hindered by a tough digital divide. Agro-tech Concern was practically unknown in remote hill and mountain districts. The farmers in these regions frequently lacked access to smartphones, had limited or no internet access, and their languages and dialects were not fully supported by the Nepali-language app. The real-time price information on the platform was also not as helpful to farmers who produced subsistence crops such as millet and buckwheat that were traded on a local scale and seldom made it to formal markets. DB Rai also admitted that Agro-tech Concern had mostly benefited commercial farmers in reachable Terai and valley districts and was yet to identify a model to reach the most marginalized agricultural groups.

The SME aspect and tourism aspect of digital transformation overlapped in the Agro-tech Concern story in a few unexpected ways. Butwal and Biratnagar. Starting with a few small hotel owners, Agro-tech Concern was used to purchase fresh local produce directly off the nearby farms, promoting farm-to-table meal service to visitors. This formed a new value chain between Nepal tourism economy and digital agriculture. A small eco resort in Annapurna Trek collaborated with Agro-tech Concern to partner with five local farmers to provide organic vegetables, and the collaboration was built into a marketing aspect to attract international tourists who were ready to pay higher prices to get the local food experience. Ministry of Agriculture also considered the possibility of collaborating with Agro-tech Concern on SME support programs since they saw as well that agricultural SMEs could enjoy access to the same digital market that it offered to individual farmers.

Different types of challenges and issues related to quality and reliability of the data on the platform are frequently arised. Data collectors at the major markets manually updated market prices, and there were frequent delays and inaccuracies. There was an instance when a wrong price list of ginger was quoted as NPR 250/kg rather than the correct NPR 150, and some farmers in Dhankuta took their produce to Dharan market, hoping to get high prices, only to lose money due to transportation expenses. Agro-tech Concern also launched a system of verification that had two data entry and automated anomaly detection, yet errors still happened, which compromised the trust of the farmers.

Sustainability in the long term also hung a cloud over the platform. Agro-tech concern was funded through a mix of donor grants provided by UNDP and Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation, government subsidies via the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock Development, and small amounts of revenue through transaction fees on the marketplace feature. The transaction charges only earned NPR 16 million per year - nowhere near it could support the platform on its own. Rai and his team were aware that the donor financing was time-limited and that the platform had to design a self-financing revenue model. Some of the options considered were premium advisory services to commercial farmers, data licensing with agribusiness firms, and a commission-based input supply option, where farmers would be able to buy seeds, fertilizers, and tools via the app.

By 2026, Agro-tech Concern had become the most popular agricultural technology platform in Nepal, but its creators realized that to achieve the next tier, some issues could only be addressed by technology, which were rural connectivity infrastructure, digital literacy among older farming communities, trustworthy data ecosystems, and a policy environment that accepted agricultural digitization as a complementary service to traditional extension services and not a primary one.
